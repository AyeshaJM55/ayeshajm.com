Good reference material reduces guessing, revisions, and the peculiar human ritual of approving details that were never documented.

## Useful source material

The ideal starting package may include:

- CAD or STEP files
- dimensioned drawings
- orthographic photographs
- close-up detail references
- material samples or finish codes
- packaging artwork
- brand guidelines

Not every project needs every item. The important part is identifying which details affect the product silhouette, construction, materials, and final usage.

## Check CAD before production

CAD files should be reviewed for missing parts, unnecessary internal geometry, inconsistent naming, and surfaces that will not render cleanly.

A production-ready visualization model often needs cleanup even when an engineering model already exists.

## Document materials clearly

Material references should describe more than color. Surface roughness, reflectivity, transparency, texture scale, and edge behavior all influence whether a digital material feels believable.

## Confirm variants early

Colorways, accessories, packaging versions, and regional differences should be identified before the scene is finalized. A small naming decision at the beginning can prevent a large folder of files called `final-final-2-revised` later.
